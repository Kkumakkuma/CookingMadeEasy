## What Kitchen Gadgets Save You Time and Money?

### Which Kitchen Gadgets Are Most Time-Saving?

Investing in the right kitchen gadgets can significantly cut your meal preparation time. Some time-saving tools I found particularly valuable include:

- **Instant Pot**: This multi-cooker can pressure cook, slow cook, sauté, steam, and more. It can reduce cooking time for dishes like stews and meats by up to 70%. For instance, a chili that would typically take 90 minutes can be done in less than 30 minutes.
- **Food Processor**: A powerful food processor like the Cuisinart DFP-14BCNY can chop, slice, and shred ingredients in seconds, which means you can prep veggies for a stir-fry in the time it would take to find your knife.
- **Electric Kettle**: If you enjoy making tea or need to boil water for pasta quickly, an electric kettle like the Cuisinart CPK-17 can bring water to a boil much faster than a stove could, usually in about 3-5 minutes.

### How Do These Gadgets Actually Cut Costs?

You might wonder how these gadgets can also help you save money. Here are a few ways they can make your wallet feel a bit heavier:

- **Reduced Food Waste**: When you can prepare meals quickly, you're less likely to let ingredients spoil. For example, having steamed veggies ready in minutes can help you consume perishables before they go bad.
- **Less Takeout**: With the ability to cook meals faster, you may find yourself less inclined to order pizza or Chinese takeout, which can cost you an average of $20-$40 per meal. Over a month, that's a potential savings of $100 or more!
- **Efficient Cooking**: When you have tools that streamline cooking, like a slow cooker or pressure cooker, you can utilize cheaper cuts of meat effectively. For instance, chicken thighs are often half the price of breasts but can deliver moist meals when cooked properly.

## What’s the Difference Between Multi-Cookers and Slow Cookers?

Many cooks often confuse multi-cookers with slow cookers. Here’s a breakdown of their key differences:

### Multi-Cookers vs. Slow Cookers

| Feature                | Multi-Cookers                                | Slow Cookers                                  |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Cooking Methods        | Pressure cook, sauté, steam, slow cook, and more | Primarily slow cooking                      |
| Cooking Time           | 70% faster cooking for some recipes         | Generally requires 4-8 hours cooking time   |
| User Friendliness      | Somewhat complex with varied settings        | Simple dial with fewer options               |
| Price Range            | $60 - $300 (like the Instant Pot Duo)       | $30 - $150 (like the Crock-Pot Classic)     |

### Which One Should You Choose?

- Opt for a **multi-cooker** if you prefer a versatile appliance that can take on different cooking methods and save time—especially useful for busy weekdays.
- Choose a **slow cooker** if you prioritize set-it-and-forget-it functionality and enjoy meals that braise over a longer period.

In my case, investing in the Instant Pot for about $130 was one of the best decisions. I often come home late, and having a gadget that pushes out a full meal in under 30 minutes has been a game changer.
